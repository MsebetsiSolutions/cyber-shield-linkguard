🧠 What is Social Engineering?

Social Engineering is when someone tricks you into giving away private information or doing something that helps them attack a system. Instead of breaking into computers, they “hack” people’s emotions and trust.

Example: An attacker sends an email pretending to be your boss:
“Please send me the password for the finance account—it’s urgent!”
If you believe it and send the password, they don’t need to hack anything — you gave it to them.

Common Tricks Attackers Use

  • Authority: Pretend to be a boss or official.
  • Urgency: Rush you to act before thinking.
  • Fear: Threaten loss of access, money, or job.
  • Curiosity: Tempt you with “shocking” or “exclusive” info.
  • Helpfulness: Pretend to need your assistance.

How to Stay Safe

  • Think before clicking links or sharing info.
  • Double-check the sender’s identity.
  • Never share passwords over email or phone.
  • Use strong passwords and two-factor authentication (2FA).
  • If something feels “off,” stop and verify.

🧩 What is an Insider Threat?

An Insider Threat is when someone inside your organization causes harm — either on purpose or by accident. It could be an employee, contractor, or even someone whose account was hacked.

Types of Insider Threats

  • Malicious Insiders: People who steal, sabotage, or leak data intentionally.
  • Careless Insiders: People who make mistakes, like clicking on phishing emails.
  • Compromised Insiders: When hackers take over an employee’s account.

Examples

  • An employee downloads company data before quitting.
  • Someone accidentally sends sensitive files to the wrong person.
  • A hacker uses a stolen login to access private systems.

How to Prevent Insider Threats

  • Give people access only to what they need (least privilege).
  • Monitor for strange activities (like late-night logins or huge downloads).
  • Train employees about cybersecurity awareness.
  • Encourage honesty — allow staff to report mistakes without punishment.
  • Use tools like UEBA (User & Entity Behavior Analytics) and DLP (Data Loss Prevention).

🌍 Why It Matters

Both social engineering and insider threats focus on people, not just technology. Firewalls and antivirus can’t protect against human mistakes — that’s why training, awareness, and trust are your strongest defenses.

“Cybersecurity isn’t just about protecting data — it’s about protecting decisions.”

📘 Module 1 — The Universal Attack Playbook

9:00–9:45 AM · Goal: Understand psychology and operational techniques attackers use — and how to detect them early.

Why 93% of breaches start with social engineering: Attackers exploit human decision shortcuts (heuristics). Instead of attacking code, they attack cognition: authority, scarcity, urgency, reciprocity, and social proof. Combine that with OSINT and simple technical tools for high success.

Psychological Triggers (Practical Notes)

  • Authority: Messages or calls that appear from senior staff.
    Control: verification codes & approval matrices.
  • Urgency: Time-limited requests that bypass normal checks.
    Control: mandatory delay & second-channel verification.
  • Reciprocity: Small favors prompt over-sharing.
    Control: limit data disclosure & train staff to refuse unsolicited requests.
