What is ISO/IEC 27001?

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS). It provides a systematic approach to managing sensitive company information, ensuring it remains secure, available, and confidential.

  • ISMS: A set of policies, procedures, and controls for managing information risks.
  • Annex A Controls: 93 security controls grouped into 4 themes: Organizational, People, Physical, and Technological.

What is ISO/IEC 27005?

ISO/IEC 27005 provides guidelines for information security risk management, supporting the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001. It helps organizations identify, assess, and treat risks to their information assets.

  • Risk Management: The process of identifying, evaluating, and mitigating risks to information assets.
  • Asset: Anything of value to the organization (data, hardware, software, people).

The Stakes: Why This Matters

Cybersecurity is not only about stopping attacks—it’s about building trust. A single oversight in information security can lead to millions in losses, service disruptions, and reputational damage. ISO 27001 and ISO 27005 are foundational for global organizations to structure their ISMS and manage risk effectively.

  • Reputational Impact: Data breaches can destroy customer trust and brand value.
  • Financial Impact: Regulatory fines, legal costs, and lost business can be severe.
  • Operational Impact: Downtime and disruption to services.
Reflection: Which sector would you trust most with your data—one that is ISO 27001 certified or one that is not? Why?

Key Terms & Acronyms

  • ISMS: Information Security Management System
  • Risk Assessment: Identifying and evaluating risks
  • Control: Safeguard or countermeasure to reduce risk
  • Residual Risk: Risk remaining after controls are applied
  • Annex A: List of controls in ISO 27001

Part 2: ISO 27001 and ISO 27005 in Practice

  • A.12.4 – Logging and Monitoring: Align log retention policies with GDPR (6 months) or CCPA (12 months).
  • A.18.2 – Compliance: Conduct third-party audits to meet overlapping requirements such as HIPAA or SOC 2.
  • A.14.2 – Secure Development: Maintain patch management programs to prevent incidents like the Log4j vulnerability.

ISO 27005: Global Risk Management Methodology

  • Asset Identification: Use NIST SP 800-30 to classify and prioritize critical infrastructure assets.
  • Threat Modeling: Apply OWASP Threat Dragon to identify potential attack vectors.
  • Risk Treatment: Transfer residual risks via cyber insurance providers such as Lloyd’s of London.

Part 3: Hands-On Lab – Building an ISMS

  1. Scenario Setup: You are part of a security team tasked with designing an ISMS for a new business unit in a global organization. Review the provided ISMS policy example and Annex A controls.
  2. Group Discussion: In your group, identify three major information assets (e.g., customer database, HR records, cloud infrastructure). For each asset, discuss potential threats and vulnerabilities using ISO 27005 risk management principles.
  3. Gap Analysis: Compare your organization’s current controls to the requirements in ISO 27001 Annex A. List any gaps and prioritize them by risk level (high, medium, low).
  4. Policy Drafting: Draft a short policy statement for one of the identified gaps. Ensure your policy includes objectives, scope, and key controls. Use clear, actionable language.
  5. Risk Register Creation: Create a simple risk register table with columns for Asset, Threat, Vulnerability, Existing Controls, Risk Level, and Recommended Treatment.
  6. Documentation: Save your gap analysis summary, policy draft, and risk register as evidence for your ISMS folder. Prepare to present your findings to the class.
  7. Troubleshooting & Reflection: If you encounter uncertainty about a control or risk, refer to the ISO 27001/27005 documentation or ask your instructor. Reflect on how the process helps organizations proactively manage information security risks.

Platform: This lab can be completed using Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, or any cloud-based document collaboration tool.

Tip: Use collaborative editing (e.g., Google Docs or Microsoft Teams) to work together and track changes in your ISMS documentation.

What is GDPR?

GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) is the European Union’s law for protecting personal data and privacy. It applies to any organization handling EU residents’ data, regardless of location.

  • Key Principles: Lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity, confidentiality, accountability.
  • Rights: Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection.

What is PIPL?

PIPL (Personal Information Protection Law) is China’s comprehensive data protection law, similar to GDPR but with stricter localization and export controls.

  • Key Principles: Legality, propriety, necessity, good faith, transparency.
  • Special Rules: CIIO (Critical Information Infrastructure Operators) must store data in China and undergo security reviews for cross-border transfers.

What is NIST SP 800-30?

NIST SP 800-30 is a foundational guide for conducting risk assessments in information systems. It provides a structured approach to identifying threats, vulnerabilities, and impacts, and is widely used in government, finance, and healthcare.

  • Risk Assessment: The process of identifying, estimating, and prioritizing risks.
  • Threat: Any circumstance or event with the potential to adversely impact operations.
  • Vulnerability: Weakness in an information system that can be exploited.

What is PCI-DSS?

PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) is a global standard for securing credit card transactions and protecting cardholder data. It applies to any organization that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder information.

  • 12 Requirements: Cover network security, access control, monitoring, and incident response.
  • Levels: Level 1 (largest merchants) to Level 4 (smallest).

Why NIST & PCI-DSS Matter

NIST SP 800-30 and PCI-DSS are global standards for risk management and payment security. They help organizations identify threats, model risks, and protect sensitive data—especially in banking, healthcare, and e-commerce.

  • Financial Impact: PCI-DSS non-compliance can result in fines up to $500,000 per incident.
  • Operational Impact: NIST risk assessments help prevent costly outages and breaches.
  • Reputational Impact: Data breaches can destroy customer trust and brand value.

Threat Modeling & Reporting

  1. STRIDE Threat Modeling (Deep Dive):
    • Choose a real-world system (e.g., mobile banking app, payment gateway, telecom portal).
    • For each STRIDE category, brainstorm and document at least two threats. Example: Spoofing – fake login page, SIM card cloning; Tampering – unauthorized API changes, transaction manipulation.
    • Map each threat to relevant NIST SP 800-30 risk assessment steps and PCI-DSS controls.
    • Use a template to record: Threat, Asset, Vulnerability, Impact, Likelihood, Control, Residual Risk.
  2. Adversary Simulation (Practical):
    • Set up MITRE Caldera or a similar adversary emulation tool. Run a simulated attack (e.g., credential theft, privilege escalation).
    • Document each attack step, detection point, and mitigation. Example: Caldera detects lateral movement; recommend network segmentation.
    • Debrief: How did the simulation change your understanding of system risk?
  3. Global Case Study (Analysis & Reporting):
    • Research a recent SIM-swapping or phishing incident (e.g., telecom breach, mobile banking fraud).
    • Draw an attack flow diagram: entry point, exploitation, impact, response.
    • Identify missed controls and propose improvements using threat modeling techniques.
    • Write a 1-page incident report: summary, root cause, lessons learned, recommended controls.
  4. Risk Appetite & Executive Reporting (Dashboard & Summary):
    • Compare risk appetite statements from financial, retail, and e-commerce sectors. Example: "We accept up to 0.1% fraud risk per quarter."
    • Build a dashboard (mockup or real) in Power BI, Tableau, or Google Sheets: show risk trends, incident rates, control effectiveness.
    • Draft a one-page executive summary: risk posture, recent incidents, mitigation status, recommendations. Use bullet points and charts for clarity.
    • Template for summary:
      • Current Risk Posture
      • Recent Incidents
      • Mitigation Actions
      • Recommendations
  5. Presentation & Reflection (Peer Review):
    • Present your threat model, case study, and dashboard to the class or group.
    • Peer review: Give and receive feedback on clarity, completeness, and actionable insights.
    • Reflect: How does advanced threat modeling and reporting improve technical and business decision-making?

Templates & Resources: STRIDE worksheet, attack flow diagram, incident report template, dashboard sample, executive summary outline.
