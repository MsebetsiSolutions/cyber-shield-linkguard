Social Media Smarts

Understand pros/cons of social media and how to create a safe, positive profile.

Quick Quiz: Digital Footprints

Which of these creates a digital footprint?

A) Posting a photo online
B) Sending a private message
C) Searching for information
D) All of the above

Correct! All online activities create digital footprints that can be tracked and stored.

Day 2

Fake News Busters

Learn to spot fake news using a simple "CRAAP Test for Kids."

What you'll learn today
  1. Why some headlines are misleading.
  2. How to use the CRAAP test: Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose — kid-friendly version.
  3. Understanding clickbait and why it's used.

Activity: "Fact or Fiction?" (interactive)

Read the headline, choose if it's Real or Fake, then click "Show Score". We'll explain the clues!

Headline A: "Aliens invited to city's school assembly — principal confirms"
Headline B: "New study shows kids who eat carrots can fly for 5 seconds"
Headline C: "Local hero wins award for cleaning up the neighbourhood park"
CRAAP Test for Kids
  • Currency: Is it new or old? Check the date.
  • Relevance: Does it matter to you? Does it sound extreme?
  • Authority: Who wrote it? Is the site real?
  • Accuracy: Are there facts and sources? Do other sites say the same?
  • Purpose: Is it trying to sell, trick, or inform?

Quick Quiz: Spotting Fake News

Which of these is a red flag for fake news?

A) The story makes you feel very angry or excited
B) It has spelling mistakes and ALL CAPS
C) You can't find the same story on other websites
D) All of the above

Correct! All of these are signs that the news might be fake. Always double-check with reliable sources.

Project: Make a "Fake News Busting" poster
Create a poster that teaches three quick checks (date, author, other sources). Display in class or hallway.

Day 3

Cyber Hygiene

Daily habits: updates, passwords, and secure websites (HTTPS).

What you'll learn today
  • Why updates and patches help fix problems.
  • How to check for secure websites (the lock icon and https://).
  • Simple password tips and when to ask an adult for help.

🔒 Cyber Hygiene Tips

1. Update Rally: Always keep your apps and devices updated

2. Password Power: Use strong, unique passwords for each account

3. HTTPS Detective: Look for the lock icon and "https://" in website addresses

4. Think Before You Click: Don't click on suspicious links or downloads

Day 4

The Ethics of Hacking

Learn about ethical hacking versus malicious hacking.

What you'll learn today
  • The difference between White Hat (good) and Black Hat (bad) hackers.
  • How ethical hackers help protect our information.
  • Why permission is important when testing security.

⚔️ White Hat vs. Black Hat Hackers

White Hat Hackers (The Good Guys):

  • Have permission to test security
  • Help fix problems they find
  • Work to protect people's information

Black Hat Hackers (The Bad Guys):

  • Break into systems without permission
  • Steal information or cause damage
  • Their actions are illegal

Activity: "White Hat vs. Black Hat"

Read these scenarios and decide if they describe a White Hat (ethical) or Black Hat (malicious) hacker.

Scenario 1: Alex finds a security problem in the school's website and immediately tells the principal.
Scenario 2: Jamie figures out a classmate's password and uses it to change their grades.
Scenario 3: Taylor is hired by a company to test their security by trying to break into their system.
The School Security Challenge

Design a simple "security system" for the classroom door and discuss how to test it without causing harm.

Quick Quiz: Ethical Hacking

What is the main difference between White Hat and Black Hat hackers?

A) White Hat hackers wear white clothes
B) Black Hat hackers are better at coding
C) White Hat hackers have permission to test security
D) There is no difference

Correct! White Hat hackers have permission to test systems, while Black Hat hackers break in without permission.

Career Spotlight: Cybersecurity Expert
Learn about careers in cybersecurity. Ethical hackers can work for companies, governments, or as independent consultants to help protect important information.
